# PDR-0003: Why Documents Are Modelled as Controlled Information Rather Than Files

Status: Accepted

## Context

Management systems rely on documented information, but certification readiness is not achieved by storing files alone. Customers need ownership, approval, revision control, distribution control, retention, evidence linkage, lifecycle state, clause mapping, audit history, and traceable publication.

Files are representations of information. They may be imported, attached, rendered, exported, archived, or distributed, but they are not sufficient as the authoritative product object.

## Decision

Certisphere will model documents as controlled information rather than files.

The authoritative domain object is controlled information with lifecycle, ownership, classification, revision, approval, relationship, evidence, retention, and audit metadata. Files are secondary assets associated with controlled information, revisions, attachments, exports, or records.

## Rationale

Controlled information reflects the real governance requirement. A procedure, policy, form, checklist, register, record, or report must be managed by its status, use, relationship, approval, and evidence context, not merely by a binary file.

This decision enables:

- Immutable approved revisions.
- Controlled publication and withdrawal.
- Clause and process traceability.
- Relationship mapping across the knowledge graph.
- Evidence linkage.
- Distribution control.
- Retention and archive rules.
- Search and impact analysis.
- Workflow-ready lifecycle transitions.

## Consequences

- The documents bounded context must preserve controlled-information concepts in domain, application, persistence, API, and UI layers.
- File upload, export, and rendering features must attach to controlled-information lifecycle rules.
- Published controlled information must remain immutable.
- Records and evidence must be modelled according to their governance purpose, not only by file type.
- Search and reporting must treat metadata, relationships, lifecycle, and evidence links as first-class data.

## Alternatives Considered

### File storage as the primary model

Rejected. It would recreate shared-drive behaviour and fail to provide enough governance for auditability and certification readiness.

### Document management system model only

Rejected. Certisphere must support integrated management-system relationships, not just document control.

### Free-form knowledge base model

Rejected. It would weaken approval, retention, distribution, revision, and evidence controls required for controlled information.
